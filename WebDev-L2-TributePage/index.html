A TRIBUTE TO A GREAT INDIAN

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man of India and the People's President

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam — scientist, teacher and former President of India.

HIS JOURNEY

A Life of Knowledge, Dreams and Service

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a modest family, he developed a strong interest in learning and science from an early age. His determination and curiosity helped him pursue education despite financial challenges.

Kalam became an important figure in India's space and defence programmes. He worked with organisations including ISRO and DRDO and contributed to major national technological programmes. His work in aerospace engineering and missile development earned him the popular title "Missile Man of India."

In 2002, he became the 11th President of India. During his presidency, he became especially popular among students and young people. He believed that education, creativity and determination could help young Indians contribute to the development of the country.

After completing his presidential term, Kalam continued to teach, write and interact with students. His books and speeches encouraged millions of young people to dream big and work towards meaningful goals. He passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong.

HIS LEGACY

Inspiring Generations to Dream

Dr. Kalam's legacy goes beyond his achievements in science and public service. He is remembered for his humility, love for education and strong belief in the potential of young people.

His life continues to inspire students, scientists, teachers and citizens to pursue knowledge and use their abilities for the betterment of society.
